2. Market size

How Big Is the Digital Bond Market?

AFME's October 2026 report puts global DLT fixed-income issuance at a record €6.4bn year to date, already 30% above the full year 2025 (€4.8bn). Asia accounted for 68% of the total, led by Hong Kong's HK$20bn (≈ €2.2bn) green bond, while platforms run by European-headquartered institutions supported about 98% of issuance. Tokenized US Treasury funds, a close cousin of digital bonds, held about $14.4bn across 119 products on 22 October 2026 (rwa.xyz data cited by Spark). Circle's USYC, Ondo's USDY and BlackRock's BUIDL were the largest at that date.

For a structural comparison of covered bonds and digital bonds, see Covered vs Digital Bonds and our Digital Bond Framework 2026.

3. Trends

Three Structural Trends in 2026

  • Central banks as issuers and testers: Hong Kong's repeated programme (now settled in tokenised deposits) and the Czech National Bank's pilot show monetary authorities moving from proofs of concept to live instruments.
  • Public blockchains enter fixed income: Solana is now used by an exchange (KASE), an asset manager (Schroders) and, from 2027, DTCC.
  • Standardisation becomes urgent: fragmentation across ledgers is the main obstacle to secondary liquidity, which is why Europe created the ETSG in November 2026.
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much digital bond issuance was there in 2026?

AFME reports a record €6.4bn of global DLT-based fixed-income issuance in 2026 year to date as of October 2026, 30% above full-year 2025.

Which central banks have issued digital bonds?

Recent examples include the Hong Kong SAR Government's digital green bond programme and the Czech National Bank, which announced its first DLT koruna bonds, tested through CNB Lab on CSD Prague's DLT Pilot Regime platform, in November 2026.

When will the UK issue its digital gilt (DIGIT)?

HM Treasury's DIGIT pilot issuance is expected by Q1 2027 on a platform operating within the Digital Securities Sandbox.
