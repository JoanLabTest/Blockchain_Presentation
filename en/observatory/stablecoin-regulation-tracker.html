This Month in Brief

November 2026 confirmed a global trend: regulators are moving stablecoins inside the banking perimeter. Taiwan wants bank-only issuance, Nigeria has licensed the activity and banned algorithmic designs, Thailand is consulting on transfer caps until 26 November and the UK is carving payment stablecoins out of the crypto-dealing regime. In Europe, the EBA has taken direct supervision of USDC, the first significant e-money token, and the bank-led Qivalis euro stablecoin is licensed and preparing to issue.

2. Analysis

Three Regulatory Patterns

  • Bank-centric issuance: Taiwan's draft and Europe's Qivalis consortium both put banks at the centre of stablecoin issuance, echoing the debate on stablecoins vs tokenized deposits.
  • AML-first controls: Thailand's transfer caps and Nigeria's monitoring rules put financial-integrity concerns ahead of innovation.
  • Payment carve-outs: the UK is separating payment stablecoins from speculative crypto activity, a step towards treating them as regulated money.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Which countries changed stablecoin rules in November 2026?

Nigeria's licensing rules took effect on 5 November, Taiwan published draft issuance rules on 3 November, Thailand opened a consultation on transfer caps closing 26 November, and the UK House of Lords approved payment-stablecoin amendments on 11 November.

Who can issue stablecoins in Taiwan?

Under the FSC's draft rules, only banks, financial holding companies or entities designated by the FSC would be allowed to issue stablecoins.

Are algorithmic stablecoins allowed in Nigeria?

No. The Central Bank of Nigeria's licensing rules, in effect since 5 November 2026, prohibit algorithmic stablecoins.

When will the Qivalis euro stablecoin launch?

Qivalis announced its e-money institution authorisation from De Nederlandsche Bank on 14 October 2026 and plans to issue within the next months, once its MiCA white paper has been filed.
