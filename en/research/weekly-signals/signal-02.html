Signal 02: The Fed Anchors Tokenization as Security Risk Resurfaces

Six signals from the week for institutional readers across tokenization, digital bonds, stablecoins, DeFi and NFTs.

Last updated: 18 November 2026 · DCM-SIG-2026-02
Signal in One Paragraph

The week's dominant theme is who anchors tokenized finance. Fed Governor Waller argued that central bank money should be the settlement anchor, DTCC committed to Solana for 2027 and Europe opened its standards group (ETSG). The $6.3bn Binance breach continues to weigh on private-market appetite for tokenization. Meanwhile, tokenized equities hit a record and retail-facing NFT infrastructure kept shrinking.

1. Policy

The Fed Wants Central Bank Money at the Core

On 17 November, Fed Governor Christopher Waller argued that central bank money should anchor tokenized finance, with private firms competing on payment features built on top. He set no timetable for connecting the Fed's payment rails with tokenized markets. The position mirrors the ECB's approach with Pontes and strengthens the case for wholesale settlement links over purely private settlement assets.

2. Infrastructure & standards

DTCC on Solana; Europe Opens the ETSG

DTCC will connect its Tokenization Service to Solana, with tokenized DTC-custodied assets expected in H2 2027 (full analysis). In Europe, the Commission, ESMA and the ECB set up the industry-led European Tokenisation Standards Group; nominations close on 27 November (full analysis).

3. Security

Binance: the Breach That Reprices Operational Risk

Attackers drained an estimated $6.3bn from Binance hot wallets on 29 October (Elliptic estimates $6.43bn), the largest crypto theft on record. TRM Labs and Chainalysis attribute it to North Korea. Binance's 3 November report describes a multi-stage campaign lasting months. About $3bn has been frozen, Binance has pledged to cover losses and withdrawals were restored in phases from 1 November. Bloomberg reports that private-market asset owners are reassessing tokenization plans as a result.

Takeaway: operational security around keys, insider access and hot-wallet exposure is now a board-level question; see our Key Infrastructure framework.

4. Markets

Tokenized Equities at a Record

Tokenized stocks and ETFs reached a record market capitalisation of roughly $4.6–4.9bn, depending on the data source. Tokenized Nvidia reached about $402m on 17 November, up 159% from 8 November, even as Nvidia's listed shares lost about $750bn in market value. The divergence suggests that on-chain demand for 24/7 equity exposure is partly decoupled from underlying price moves.

5. DeFi & NFT

Fixed-Term Yield Arrives; NFT Marketplaces Consolidate

DeFi: Pendle and Ethena launched four fixed-term USDe markets on Ethereum (3, 6, 9 and 12 months) on 10 November, with initial yields of 5.24%–6.00%. Liquidity was thin at about $10k per pool. It is a DeFi analogue of the bond curve and worth watching alongside the Digital Bond Tracker.

NFT: Blur's front-end shut down on 12 November and its Blend lending protocol stopped accepting new loans. Weekly NFT sales were $35.1m (+6% week on week). One dataset puts NFT volume at $6.37bn in 2024, $2.68bn in 2025 and $0.39bn in 2026 up to 13 October. Only 21 of 56 early marketplaces still trade, and NFT Paris was cancelled for 2026. NFT trading is turning into a feature of wallets and broader trading apps rather than a standalone market.
