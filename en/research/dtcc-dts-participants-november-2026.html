DTCC DTS: 20 Institutions Deploy

First participant cohort analysis of the DTCC Digital Tokenization Service — how Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Citi, BofA, Fidelity and Citadel Securities are structuring tokenized security positions on Canton Network.

Executive Summary

As of November 2026, twenty major financial institutions have joined the DTCC Digital Tokenization Service (DTS). The first live cohort includes Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Citi, Bank of America, Fidelity and Citadel Securities, deploying tokenized Russell 1000 equities, index ETFs and U.S. Treasuries. The SEC's December 2025 no-action letter provides the regulatory foundation.

1. Participant Architecture

How the First Cohort Structures Tokenized Positions

At the core of the DTCC Digital Tokenization Service is a custody-first architecture: the DTCC's depository subsidiary creates digital tokens that represent securities it already holds on behalf of participants, rather than moving underlying assets to a separate blockchain-native custodian. This design preserves the existing ownership rights and investor protections associated with the conventional securities framework — participants retain the same legal entitlements to dividends, voting rights, and principal as they would under traditional book-entry ownership. The token is not a parallel instrument but a programmable representation of a position that already exists within DTCC's established clearing infrastructure.

A critical feature of the DTS architecture is bidirectional convertibility between tokens and conventional securities. Participants can at any time convert tokenized positions back into standard book-entry form, and vice versa, without settlement disruption or change in custodial status. This interoperability between the tokenized and traditional rails reduces entry barriers for institutions that wish to test digital asset capabilities incrementally, without committing to a wholesale migration. Tokens are transferable to institutional digital wallets that meet DTCC's eligibility criteria, enabling the kind of programmable collateral mobility — same-day movement, automated margining — that is central to the service's value proposition for sell-side and buy-side participants alike.

The twenty founding participants access the service primarily through Canton Network, the permissioned blockchain infrastructure developed by Digital Asset, which is designed for institutional interoperability across independently governed nodes. A private Hyperledger Besu network operated by DTCC provides an additional connectivity layer for participants requiring a more controlled environment. Morgan Stanley, one of the named early adopters, has indicated through its Head of Digital Asset Strategy, Amy Oldenburg, that this live operational experience is intended to build the concrete institutional knowledge base that will shape the firm's future digital asset capabilities — a signal that the DTS is being treated not as a one-time experiment but as the foundation of a durable operational competency.

2. Market Implications

From Pilot to Production: The Balance Sheet Impact

The DTCC has articulated several specific efficiency claims for the Digital Tokenization Service that go beyond the general narrative of blockchain-enabled settlement. At the balance sheet level, the service is expected to improve liquidity by enabling faster and more granular collateral transfers — particularly relevant for repo desks and prime brokerage operations where intraday margin calls require rapid mobilisation of eligible securities. The programmable nature of tokenized positions allows collateral to move between counterparties without the conventional settlement lag, reducing the volume of assets that must be pre-positioned overnight as a buffer against timing risk. For large institutions managing hundreds of billions in posted collateral, even incremental improvements in collateral velocity carry meaningful capital efficiency implications.

The broader context of real-world asset (RWA) tokenization provides useful scale reference for the DTS launch. According to data from RWA.xyz, the total on-chain RWA market had reached approximately $39 billion by late 2026, with tokenized U.S. Treasuries accounting for approximately $14 billion of that figure — the single largest asset class within the broader tokenized securities ecosystem. The DTCC's entry into live production, with twenty institutional participants and three asset classes from day one, positions the DTS as a systemically significant addition to this market. Unlike many earlier tokenization initiatives that remained within single-institution perimeters or bilateral arrangements, the DTS operates at the level of market infrastructure shared across the industry, which amplifies both its potential network effects and the regulatory scrutiny it will attract in subsequent phases.

The strategic horizon for the DTS extends beyond its current Canton Network and Besu perimeter. DTCC has publicly indicated its intention to integrate additional blockchain networks in 2027, a roadmap that reflects the broader industry consensus that institutional tokenization will not consolidate around a single chain but will instead require interoperability frameworks capable of connecting multiple permissioned and potentially hybrid environments. For the twenty founding participants, the period from Q4 2026 through 2027 represents a first-mover window in which operational experience with tokenized securities — across settlement workflows, risk systems, legal documentation, and client reporting — will translate into competitive differentiation. The transition from pilot to production is now complete; the next determinant of market structure will be the speed at which the remaining institutional universe moves from observation to participation.
