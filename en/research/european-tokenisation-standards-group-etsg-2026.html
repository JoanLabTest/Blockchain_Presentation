Europe Opens the Standards Room: the European Tokenisation Standards Group

The European Commission, ESMA and the ECB have created an industry-led group to harmonise standards for tokenised securities and DLT market infrastructures. Nominations close on 27 November 2026.

Last updated: 18 November 2026 · DCM-REG-2026-010
Executive Summary

Through a mandate dated 9 November 2026, the European Commission, ESMA and the ECB established the European Tokenisation Standards Group (ETSG). The group is chaired by industry representatives, with the three authorities acting as observers. Its task is to review existing European and international tokenisation standards, identify gaps and determine where additional standards are needed to make DLT market infrastructures interoperable. The call for nominations closes on 27 November 2026, and initial standards are expected by early 2028.

1. What was announced

A Public–Private Workstream for Tokenised Markets

On 9 November 2026, ESMA published two documents: the Mandate of the European Tokenisation Standards Group and a Background and Call for nominations. Together they set up the ETSG as a forum where market participants define the standards needed for tokenised securities to circulate across DLT infrastructures. The European Commission, ESMA and the ECB remain in an observer role.

According to the mandate and press coverage, the group will (i) map existing European and international standards relevant to tokenisation, (ii) identify gaps and (iii) recommend where additional standards are needed. Initial deliverables are expected in early 2028.

2. Why it matters

Interoperability Is Now the Binding Constraint

Europe already has a legal sandbox for tokenised securities, the DLT Pilot Regime, but infrastructures remain siloed. Each DLT market infrastructure uses its own token standards, identifiers and settlement-asset conventions. Without common standards, liquidity fragments and every new venue has to build bilateral bridges.

The authorities want this standards work to begin before co-legislators finalise the amendments to the DLT Pilot Regime. Those amendments were proposed in December 2025 as part of the Market Integration and Supervision Package and are still going through the legislative process. ESMA's review of the regime, published in June 2025, had already opened the door to extending, amending or making it permanent.

For issuers and investors, this links directly to the settlement-asset debate: wholesale central bank money (see ECB Pontes), tokenised deposits and MiCA-compliant stablecoins.

3. Who should apply

Profiles the ETSG Will Need

  • DLT market infrastructures and CSDs operating or preparing a DLT TSS / DLT MTF licence
  • Issuers and arrangers active in digital bond issuance
  • Custodians and tokenisation platforms, who own key-management and asset-servicing problems
  • Standard-setters and trade bodies (identifiers, messaging, smart-contract standards)
  • Legal and compliance experts on finality, settlement and the MiCA/MiFID perimeter

Participation is open to EU associations, private entities, global institutions, fintechs and technical providers, but each nominee must represent an entity with a European presence. The authorities hold observer roles without voting rights, and all candidates receive feedback on their nomination. Check the full criteria in ESMA's call before 27 November 2026.

4. DCM Core view

What to Watch Through 2028

  • Composition: the balance between banks, crypto-native platforms and market infrastructures will shape the outcome.
  • Settlement asset: whether the standards stay neutral or favour central bank money.
  • Global alignment: whether the work converges with US efforts (DTCC's multichain Tokenization Service) and with UK DIGIT.
  • Legislative sequencing: how ETSG output is reflected in the final amended DLT Pilot Regime.
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the European Tokenisation Standards Group (ETSG)?

The ETSG is an industry-led group set up by the European Commission, ESMA and the ECB to review existing tokenisation standards, identify gaps and recommend additional standards for interoperability between DLT market infrastructures.

What is the ETSG application deadline?

According to ESMA's call for nominations and press coverage, applications to join the ETSG must be submitted by 27 November 2026.

When will the ETSG publish standards?

The group is expected to start work in January 2027 and deliver its first set of standards by early 2028.

How does the ETSG relate to the DLT Pilot Regime?

The authorities want standards work to start before co-legislators finalise the December 2025 proposal to amend the DLT Pilot Regime, part of the Market Integration and Supervision Package.

Who chairs the ETSG?

Industry representatives chair the group. The European Commission, ESMA and the ECB participate as observers.
