Stablecoins as
Settlement Infrastructure

Structural analysis of USDT and USDC. Beyond crypto liquidity, these digital IOUs act as a global settlement infrastructure for cross-border institutional flows (Off-CBDC).

USDT (Tether)

Issued by Tether Limited (offshore), USDT is the dominant asset in terms of global liquidity. Although ubiquitous in emerging-market trading flows, its risk profile raises institutional concerns.

  • Reserves: Mix of T-Bills, commercial paper, secured loans, bitcoin and gold. Perceived lack of strict transparency (attestations rather than full audits).
  • Deployment: Multichain (Ethereum, Tron, Solana). Strong footprint on Tron for emerging cross-border B2B settlement.
  • Institutional risk: Regulatory uncertainty and counterparty risk (Centralized Issuer).

USDC (Circle)

Issued by Circle, a US-regulated entity. USDC was designed specifically as a compliant liquidity bridge, interfacing directly with the US banking system (BNY Mellon, BlackRock).

  • Reserves: Backed exclusively by cash (USD) and short-term US Treasuries (T-Bills). High on-chain transparency.
  • Compliance: Proven ability to blacklist sanctioned addresses directly (OFAC).
  • Institutional risk: Strong adoption by institutional DeFi players, but it remains a centralised authority (Centralized Issuer).

Insight Governance OS : Fiat-backed stablecoins are not decentralised currencies. They are Digital IOUs (acknowledgements of debt) managed by smart contracts. Counterparty risk rests entirely on the centralised issuer.

Monetary Contraction Mechanics (Mint / Burn)

Unlike L1 blockchains where issuance (block reward) is algorithmic, control of the supply of a fiat stablecoin rests on the issuer's discretionary authority, depending on fiat liquidity inflows or withdrawals.

Modelling Financial Flows (Fee Mechanics)

A. Network Gas Fees

Paid in the native token (ETH, SOL, TRX) for every USDC/USDT transfer. These fees go directly to the Validators / Miners of the underlying network.

B. Issuer Fees

Discretionary fees charged by Circle/Tether in the direct process of Mint (creation) or Redemption (fiat redemption).

C. Yield on Reserves

Interest earned on the T-Bills held in reserve (5%+) is not distributed to USDC holders. They are the issuer's exclusive revenue.
