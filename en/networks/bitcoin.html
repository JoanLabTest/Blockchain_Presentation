Network Profile

Bitcoin deliberately prioritises Censorship resistance and immutability monetary at the expense of processing speed (throughput) and smart contract expressiveness (Turing-incomplete).

Scalability Trilemma & Layer 2

Bitcoin opts for maximum decentralisation and maximum security, sacrificing scalability (7 TPS) on the main chain (L1). To work around this, high-frequency transactions and micro-payments are moved to the Lightning Network (L2), which uses P2P payment channels for near-infinite scalability without congesting the main blockchain.

Technical Architecture

Unlike the model of accounts (account-based) used by Ethereum or banks, Bitcoin uses the model UTXO (Unspent Transaction Output).

UTXO Model

There is no global stored "balance". Your balance is the sum of the fractions of bitcoin (UTXOs) you have received but not yet spent. This model is highly verifiable and favours parallel computation.

Difficulty Adjustment

Every 2,016 blocks (~2 weeks), the protocol adjusts the cryptographic difficulty required to mine a block, so that the average block time stays at 10 minutes whatever hardware is deployed.

Mining & Security (PoW)

Proof-of-Work consensus is Bitcoin's thermodynamic anchor. Miners spend real physical energy to generate cryptographic hashes.

Attack Vector (51%)

To censor or alter the Bitcoin blockchain, an attacker would have to acquire and power more than 51% of global hashing capacity. The capital cost (ASICs) and operating cost (electricity) make such an attack economically suicidal and logistically near impossible, even for a state, today.

Hard Money Policy

Bitcoin issuance is mathematically predetermined. Every 210,000 blocks (~4 years), the block reward is halved. This is the Halving.

Stock-to-Flow

By reducing the new flow of BTC, Bitcoin increases its stock-to-flow ratio, theoretically making it "harder" and scarcer than physical gold over time.

Governance & Risks (BIPs)

Bitcoin governance happens outside the protocol. Decisions require strong social consensus among developers (Core Devs), miners and nodes, through formal proposals BIPs (Bitcoin Improvement Proposals).

These dynamics make Bitcoin extremely slow to change, which is seen as a flaw for software innovation but as an absolute virtue for the resilience of a monetary layer.

Hashrate Centralisation

Although nodes validate consensus, the mining industry is concentrated. At times, the two largest mining pools (Foundry USA & AntPool) have controlled more than 50% of global hashrate, creating a potential (if theoretical) vector for collusion or regulatory censorship.
